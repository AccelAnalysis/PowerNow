# Chapter 4 — The Big Three for Today

You are not just a business owner. You’re a whole person.

That sounds obvious, but most of us don’t plan our days like it’s true. We plan like our only job is to grind for the business—and then we’re surprised when there’s nothing left for the people and passions that are supposed to make all this effort worth it.

That’s where the Big Three come in.

---

## The Big Three for a Whole Life

When I say “Big Three,” I don’t mean, “Pick three things for the whole day and that’s it.”

I mean:

* **Three Bigs for your business**
* **Three Bigs for your personal life**

Six total, but chosen with *one critical constraint*:

> All six have to realistically fit inside the time window you’re giving yourself today.

That window is everything.

You might only have:

* 3 focused hours before work,
* 90 minutes between meetings,
* or 2 hours after the kids go to bed.

The point is not to cram in as much as humanly possible; it’s to choose what *truly matters* for this specific day, inside the time you actually have—not the fantasy version of your calendar where nothing interrupts you and you somehow have the energy level of a teenager on espresso.

So when you choose your Big Three for business and your Big Three for life, you’re not just choosing tasks. You’re designing a day that respects your **limits**, your **priorities**, and your **future self**.

---

## Why the Timing of “Now” Matters So Much

Here’s where “now” becomes more than a motivational word.

If you go to work first thing in the morning and you tell yourself,
“I’ll get to my Big Three for the business later,”
what usually happens?

“Later” keeps moving.

You respond to emails, jump on calls, handle fires, say yes to random requests, and let other people’s urgency write your schedule. By the time you look up, it’s late afternoon and your brain is mush. Now you’re trying to start your real work when your fuel tank is on E.

And guess what happens next?

Because you didn’t move the business Big Three when you were fresh, they bleed into the time that should belong to:

* your family,
* your friends,
* your body,
* your faith,
* your hobbies,
* your rest.

You may still “get things done,” but there’s a cost:

* Your business work feels heavier than it needs to.
* Your family gets the leftovers.
* Your passions become “someday.”
* You start to resent both work *and* home because they keep colliding.

This is why **NOW** isn’t just about speed. It’s about **protecting the boundaries between your roles**.

Putting your business Big Three earlier in your day (in whatever “early” looks like for your life) isn’t just a productivity move. It’s a protection move. It protects:

* Your energy for the most important work, and
* Your evenings and weekends for the most important people.

---

## Why Three? The Balance Between Fire and Fuel

Three is not a magic number.

I am not claiming that research has discovered that exactly three priorities is biologically optimal for every person, job, or day.

The **Big Three** is a practical constraint.

It is small enough to force prioritization and large enough to let you create meaningful movement across more than one important area.

You might be thinking, “Why three? Why not five? Why not ten if I’m really trying to crush it?”

Because being able to execute *now* is not just about putting all the energy in the world behind your work in one fiery burst. It’s about having the energy to keep doing what you’re doing at a **sustainable pace**.

Three sits in a powerful tension:

* **Not too much.**

  * If you overload your list, you burn through willpower in guilt and decision fatigue before you even start.
  * You end up busy, scattered, and frustrated when half the list rolls over to tomorrow… again.

* **Not too little.**

  * If you set the bar too low—“Just one big thing”—you might protect your ego from failure, but you also starve yourself of the *good kind* of pressure.
  * You miss out on the sense of momentum that comes from moving several meaningful pieces forward.

Three is intentional. It says:

* “I am not going to pretend I can do everything.”
* “I am not going to hide and do nothing.”
* “I’m going to pick a few things that matter and give them real focus.”

That choice feeds two things your drive depends on:

1. **The feeling of accomplishment.**
   You get to look back and say, “I actually moved something important today.” That’s fuel.

2. **The relief of not feeling defeated.**
   You’re not staring at a list of 27 unchecked boxes wondering what’s wrong with you. You gave yourself a winnable game—and you played it.

Three gives you enough challenge to stay engaged but not so much load that you’re crushed. Over time, that balance compounds into confidence.

---

## How to Choose the Right Business Three

Let’s start on the work side.

Your **Big Three for business** are not just “things you could do.” They should be:

* Directly tied to the version of success you defined earlier in the book.
* Real actions, not vague intentions.
* Possible within the time and energy you actually have today.

Ask yourself:

> “If I only got three business things done today, which three would make this a genuine win for the business?”

Often, they fall into categories like:

* Revenue: proposals, sales calls, follow-ups.
* Systems: documenting a process, setting up a tool, delegating a repeatable task.
* Visibility: content, outreach, networking.
* Delivery: serving current clients with excellence.

A weak Big Three item:

> “Work on marketing.”

A strong Big Three item:

> “Record and upload one 90-second video answering a common client question.”

One is a fog. The other is a target.

And remember: these three must **fit inside your actual work window.** If you only have two focused 45-minute slots today, don’t choose three tasks that each require two hours of deep work. That’s not ambitious; that’s self-sabotage with good intentions.

---

## How to Choose the Right Personal Three

Your **Big Three for life** matter just as much—and honestly, even more in the long run.

If your business thrives but your marriage, health, friendships, or sense of purpose fall apart, you haven’t won. You’ve just traded one kind of loss for another.

So once you’ve chosen your three for business, ask:

> “What three personal actions would make today a win for me as a whole person?”

Examples might look like:

* Health: “Take a 25-minute walk,” “Prep a healthy lunch instead of grabbing fast food.”
* Relationships: “Call Mom,” “Read with my child for 15 minutes,” “Ask my spouse one real question and listen.”
* Soul/Inner Life: “10 minutes of prayer or meditation,” “Journal three bullet points about what I’m grateful for.”
* Passion: “Spend 20 minutes writing music,” “Sketch for 15 minutes,” “Read one chapter of a book I enjoy.”

Again, three isn’t about perfection. It’s about **presence**.

You’re telling yourself:

> “My life is not only about invoices and emails. My life includes my people, my health, my growth—and they get space on the board.”

---

## Fitting It All Inside the Window

So you have:

* 3 business Bigs
* 3 personal Bigs

Now you place them inside your **time window.**

Maybe your day looks like this:

* **6:30–7:30 AM** – Personal Big Three (quiet, before the world wakes up)
* **9:00–12:00 PM** – 2 of your Business Big Three (deep work focus)
* **3:00–3:30 PM** – Last Business Big (follow-ups, quick action)
* **6:00–8:00 PM** – Family time, where one of your personal Bigs lives (like playing a game with your kids or going on a walk with your spouse)

This doesn’t have to be rigid, but it *does* have to be honest:

* Honest about how long things actually take.
* Honest about how much mental energy different tasks require.
* Honest about what time of day you’re sharpest for business vs. most present for people.

If you consistently run out of time, that’s not a moral failure. It’s feedback. It means:

* Your Big Three for that area are too big,
* Or you’re trying to fit them into a time window that doesn’t really exist.

The fix isn’t to give up; it’s to **resize the work or resize the day.**

## The Evidence Behind Structuring Your Time

There is evidence behind the broader idea here, even if the exact number three is our practical choice.

Brad Aeon, Aïda Faber, and Alexandra Panaccio conducted a meta-analysis of **158 studies** on time management, covering 490 effect sizes and more than 53,000 participants. They found that time management was moderately related to job performance, academic achievement, and well-being, and negatively related to distress. Their definition of time management centered on behaviors such as **structuring, protecting, and adapting time**. (Aeon, Faber, & Panaccio, 2021)

That is an important distinction.

Time management does not create more hours.

It helps determine what a fixed number of hours will be allowed to contain.

The Big Three works in that spirit.

You are not trying to make the day bigger.

You are deciding, in advance, what deserves a protected place inside the day you actually have.

And because task switching carries measurable costs, as we saw in Chapter 2, placing meaningful work into real blocks can also reduce the amount of time lost repeatedly reorienting between competing tasks.

So the evidence-based claim is not:

> “Three tasks is scientifically perfect.”

It is:

> **Structuring and protecting time around clear priorities is associated with better performance and well-being, and unnecessary switching has a cost.**

The Big Three is the simple way we will put that principle to work.

## The Emotional Side: Accomplishment vs. Defeat

There’s a quiet battle happening inside you every day:

> “Am I someone who follows through or someone who always falls short?”

Your brain is keeping score, whether you acknowledge it or not.

When your daily expectations are unrealistic, you train yourself to feel **defeated**:

* “I never get it all done.”
* “I’m always behind.”
* “What’s the point of planning if it never goes the way I want?”

But when you choose a realistic, meaningful Big Three for business and life—and you actually complete most or all of them—you train yourself to feel **capable**:

* “I say I’ll do things and then I do them.”
* “Every day I chip away at the important stuff.”
* “I can be trusted with bigger goals because I’m faithful with today’s.”

That feeling of accomplishment is not ego fluff. It’s the fuel for tomorrow’s action.

At the same time, a reasonable Big Three protects you from the kind of self-inflicted defeat that slowly erodes your confidence. You’re not giving yourself an impossible exam every day. You’re giving yourself a winnable game that still stretches you.

## The Big Picture: This Is How NOW Becomes a Lifestyle

The point of the Big Three isn’t to create yet another productivity system to admire and then ignore.

It’s to give your **now** a structure that:

* Respects all parts of who you are,
* Moves your business forward,
* Honors your people and your body,
* And leaves you with enough energy to come back and do it again tomorrow.

You’re not just a business owner.
You’re not just a parent.
You’re not just a spouse, a friend, a dreamer, a leader.

You’re all of those at once.

The Big Three for business and the Big Three for life are how you make sure that *today* reflects that reality—not someday when things “calm down,” not after the big launch, not after the kids are older.

Today.

This is where Book 1 has been trying to bring you.

You defined the win.

You identified the work that moves it.

You mapped the goal through Start → Sustain → Succeed.

And now you have given **today** a small number of clear actions that deserve your attention.

That is clarity doing what it is supposed to do:

**making action easier to see.**
